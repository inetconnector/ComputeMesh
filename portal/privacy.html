Privacy Policy / Datenschutzhinweise

Effective: 27 August 2026 · This notice describes the intended ComputeMesh processing framework. Actual production configuration and processor contracts must remain aligned with this notice.

1. Controller

Controller (Verantwortlicher): Herbert Daniel Frede, InetConnector.com, Bismarckstraße 6, 97209 Veitshöchheim, Germany. Email: privacy@inetconnector.com. Further contact details are available in the Impressum. This address is the privacy contact; it is not represented as a formally appointed Data Protection Officer unless such an appointment is separately published.

2. Categories of data

Depending on how you use the Service, we may process account and contact data; authentication and security data; API/request routing metadata; IP addresses, timestamps and technical logs; billing, invoice and transaction metadata; provider node identifiers, hardware/telemetry and payout destination information; support communications; and content you submit for inference together with generated responses to the extent technically necessary to perform the request.

3. Purposes and legal bases

We process data to provide and administer the Service and accounts, route and meter workloads, bill customers and settle providers, provide support, secure and prevent abuse of the network, comply with legal obligations, and establish or defend legal claims. Depending on the processing, the legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (contract/pre-contract measures), Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR (legal obligation), Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interests in secure, reliable and economically operated services, fraud prevention and legal defense), or Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR where consent is required. Where we process data solely on behalf of a business customer, the allocation of controller/processor roles may instead be governed by an Art. 28 GDPR data-processing agreement.

4. AI prompts and outputs

ComputeMesh is designed to minimize persistent storage of prompt and output content in the inference path. However, we do not promise an absolute "zero log" condition across every component, third-party dependency, error condition or customer-selected configuration. Content may be transiently processed in memory and transmitted to the compute resources required to perform the request. Security, abuse handling, debugging or legal obligations may require limited processing of metadata and, only where necessary and configured, content. Customers must not submit personal data, special-category data, secrets or confidential information unless they have determined that the selected configuration, legal basis and contractual safeguards are appropriate.

5. Recipients and independent providers

Data may be disclosed where necessary to hosting/infrastructure providers, payment providers, communications/support providers, professional advisers and public authorities where legally required. Distributed compute providers may technically process workload data required for assigned jobs. Provider access is intended to be technically restricted, but no distributed system can be represented as eliminating every security risk. Business customers are responsible for selecting appropriate deployment/data-residency options for their use case.

6. Payments

Payment transactions may be processed by Stripe or another payment provider shown at checkout. Payment providers process payment and anti-fraud data under their own terms and privacy information and/or as processors depending on the activity. ComputeMesh should not receive full raw payment-card credentials where hosted payment interfaces are used. Billing and transaction records may be retained as required by tax, commercial and accounting law.

7. Provider identity, telemetry and payouts

For hardware providers we may process node identity, public cryptographic identifiers, IP/network data, hardware characteristics, availability, job/verification telemetry, earnings, settlement records, tax/accounting information and payout destination data. Private cryptographic keys are intended to remain on the provider device; users remain responsible for safeguarding keys under their control.

8. Server logs and security

Web and API infrastructure may generate security and access logs, including IP address, timestamp, request path, response status, user agent and security events. Such logs are processed for operational security, troubleshooting, abuse prevention and legal defense. Retention is limited to what is necessary for those purposes, subject to longer retention where an incident, legal claim or statutory duty requires it.

9. Retention

We retain personal data only as long as necessary for the relevant purpose and thereafter where statutory retention periods or legal-claim periods apply. Account data is generally retained for the contractual relationship; billing/accounting records for applicable statutory periods; security records for a proportionate security period; and support/legal records as necessary to resolve the matter. Data is deleted or anonymized when no longer required, unless continued retention is legally permitted or required.

10. International transfers

Some service providers may process data outside the European Economic Area. Where GDPR Chapter V requires safeguards, transfers must rely on an applicable adequacy decision, approved standard contractual clauses or another lawful transfer mechanism, together with supplementary measures where required. The exact mechanism depends on the provider and production configuration.

11. Cookies, local storage and external resources

The legal pages are designed not to require external web-font requests. The wider portal may use technically necessary browser storage for session, security or preference functions. Non-essential analytics, advertising or similar tracking must not be enabled for users requiring consent unless an appropriate consent mechanism and corresponding disclosure are in place. Production deployments must keep this section synchronized with the actual cookie/storage inventory.

12. Security

We use technical and organizational measures appropriate to the risk, which may include transport encryption, authentication, access controls, key isolation, logging, network restrictions and encrypted storage. No Internet or distributed-compute system is completely secure; therefore absolute security, confidentiality or uninterrupted availability cannot be guaranteed.

13. Your GDPR rights

Subject to the statutory requirements, data subjects may have rights of access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20), objection (Art. 21), and withdrawal of consent with future effect (Art. 7(3)). You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a competent data-protection supervisory authority. Requests may be sent to privacy@inetconnector.com. Identity verification may be required where necessary to protect account data.

14. Automated decision-making

ComputeMesh inference produces automated outputs, but the Operator does not intend account administration itself to make decisions based solely on automated processing that produce legal effects or similarly significantly affect individuals within Art. 22 GDPR, unless separately disclosed and lawfully implemented. Customers using AI outputs in their own decision processes are responsible for assessing their own Art. 22 and AI-regulatory obligations.

15. Changes

This notice may be updated when processing, providers, law or production architecture changes. Material changes will be published in an appropriate manner. The production system, records of processing activities, processor agreements and this notice should be reviewed together before commercial launch and after material architectural changes.
